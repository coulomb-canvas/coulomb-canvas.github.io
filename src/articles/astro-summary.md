### Are We at the Centre?

No.

More formally: the two foundational assumptions of modern cosmology are that the universe is **isotropic** (looks the same in every direction) and **homogeneous** (looks the same from every location). There is no preferred direction and no preferred place. Every observer, everywhere in the universe, would see all other galaxies receding from them in exactly the same way. Nobody is special.

It can be shown that Hubble's Law — recession speed proportional to distance — is the *only* expansion law consistent with these two assumptions. So the fact that it's linear is not a coincidence. It's a direct consequence of the universe having no centre.

Einstein, who constructed a static model of the universe before Hubble's discovery (introducing a fudge factor called the cosmological constant to keep it static), reportedly called it his "greatest blunder" when the expansion was discovered. Whether he was more annoyed at missing the prediction of expansion, or at being wrong, history doesn't record.
