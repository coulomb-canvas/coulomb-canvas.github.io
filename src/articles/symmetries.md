In particle physics, a symmetry is an operation you apply to the system which leaves the system unchanged. Take a square for example. Rotate it 90 degrees. Has it changed? Not really. So 90 degree rotation is a symmetry of a square.

There are 3 flavours of symmetry:

### Continuous Symmetries

#### Time

Take an experiment, say the Michaelson Morley experiment why not.  Let's have the experiment begin at time \\(t=0\\). We of course get the LOL-worthy result of no shift in the diffraction pattern, which means no luminiferous aether.

Now they did the experiment just out of curiosity, but what they didn't know is that, in the year 2400 AD, this discovery that there was no luminiferous aether would somehow cause the universe to end. Luckily, right before the universe ends, a couple of survivors manage to build a time machine and in Yu-Gi-5Ds / Inazuma Eleven GO Chronos Stones fashion, travel back in time to stop this discovery being made.

Now the easy option would be to just kill Michaelson and Morley so that they can't do the experiment, but that wouldn't make for a very interesting plot, so instead they do the typical "I can travel to any point in time to try and stop you, yet I pick the moment RIGHT before it's too lake" strategy. They meet Michaelson and Morley and distract them for a little bit, in hopes that if the experiment were to take place at a different time, the result would be different.

They were able to distract them for about 50 seconds. That means the experiment now begins at time \\(t=50\\). And they run the experiment aaaand... no shift in the diffraction pattern, no luminiferous aether, skip a few steps and hey ho, the universe has ended.

Clearly they failed, so they try again, but this time help Michaelson and Morley set up the experiment, such that it cuts off 2 and a half minutes of setting up, meaning they start the experiment 2 and a half minutes earlier \\(t=-150\\). They run the experiment and... womp womp.

What does this stupid little story have to do with symmetries? What it tells us is that all times are indistinguishable, meaning that it doesn't matter *when* you do the experiment, the result will be the same (assuming any other conditions don't change).

### Internal symmetry

Internal symmetries, or gauge symmetries, are the most physically useless symmetries, since they are pretty much only mathematical. But they exist so guess we have to mention 'em too.

- Wavefunction phase: you can quite easily prove that the probability density of a function doesn't change with phase
- Upness and downness or quarks: Up quarks and down quarks have the same physics
- Colour: the colour of a particle doesn't change the physics of the system

### Discrete symmetry

Kylo and Lusine return to the future, upset about the fact that nothing they did worked. They bring out their secret weapon: the discrete-symmetry-inator! A powerful tool which can shift the dynamics of the whole universe.

#### Parity (\\(\\hat{P}\\))

The discrete-symmetry-inator has 3 different buttons: the first of which has a P with a circumflex (\\(\\hat{P}\\)). This represents *parity*. Parity means taking the whole universe and flipping it like a pancake. So left is right, right is left. All position and momentum vectors become negative.

Under parity, \\( \\vec{r} \\to -\\vec{r} \\) and \\( \\vec{p} \\to -\\vec{p} \\)

So right before the experiment is conducted, they press the \\(\\hat{P}\\) button to pancake-ify the universe. Hopefully that should do something, right?

Guess what?! The experiment worked exactly the same, again! 

#### Charge conjugation (\\(\\hat{C}\\))

The second button had a C with a circumflex (\\(\\hat{C}\\)), for *charge conjugation*. This means that every single charge and quantum number flips. So positive charges become negative, negative charges become positive. Particles become antiparticles, antiparticles become particle. Baryon numbers switch, lepton numbers switch, the whole universe is turned inside out.

And you'd think that surely this would do *something*, right? So Lusine takes the discrete-symmetry-inator, and presses the \\(\\hat{C}\\) button. The experiment is rerun... and the result is still the exact same!!

#### Time Reversal (\\(\\hat{T}\\))

At this point, Kylo and Lusine has run out of options, so

Time reversal is exactly what it sounds like, run time backwards. The main idea is that if you had two reactants becoming two products (so something like \\( A + B \\to C + D \\)) and this reaction has a 30% chance of happening, then by running the reaction in reverse (getting the reactants back from the products \\( C + D \\to A + B \\)), it would have the same 30% probability.

#### The weak force 

Now remember that in this particular case, we've been referring to the diffraction of light waves, which involves the electromagnetic force. Therefore in this case, the electromagnetic force doesn't change when you pancake the universe, or when you flip it inside out. Moreover running the experiment in reverse undoes the effect.

This is also true of the strong force (and gravity too probably), but it's not actually always true. God knows how they measured this, but somehow particle physicists were able to prove that if you pancake the universe, the weak force actually *does* change (i.e. the weak force is NOT parity-invariant). That means some of the interactions which take place in our universe couldn't take place if the universe was mirrored.

In fact, the weak force is a bit of a rebel, because not only does it choose not to comply in a mirrored universe, but it actually doesn't comply with any of these transformations. Again, God knows how they measured this, but if you replace all positive charge with negative charge, the weak force behaves differently. And if you reverse time, the probability of getting the reactants back is different to if you were to run the reaction in the forward direction.

As a matter of fact, even if Kylo and Lusine were to press the \\(\\hat{P}\\) button and the \\(\\hat{C}\\) button at the same time (\\(\\hat{P}\\hat{C}\\), meaning the universe is both pancaked and antiparticle-ified), the weak interaction still isn't the same. The only way you can get the weak force to listen is if you combine all 3 of the transformations (\\(\\hat{P}\\hat{C}\\hat{T}\\), a pancaked, antiparticle-ified and time reversed universe). 

Now as it stands, quantum theory is defined in such a way that all forces MUST abide by the \\(\\hat{P}\\hat{C}\\hat{T}\\) rule, including the weak force. For some reason the weak force took grave offence to this and decided to ignore everything else. God knows why, but it just does.
