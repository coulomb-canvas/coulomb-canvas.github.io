### Beta Decay

<p>The easiest to understand of the three, and also most common, is the beta decay. When a neutron decays into a proton, it also releases an electron (to conserve charge) and an electron antineutrino (to conserve lepton number)</p> \[ n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e \]

This process is called *beta decay* (specifically *beta minus decay*), since the electron in this case is called a *beta particle*. I believe it's called that since this is the exact process responsible for *beta decay*.

<p>Protons can also experience beta decay, decaying into a neutron, a positron and an electron neutrino, a process known as <em>beta plus decay</em>. That might come as a surprise, since neutrons are heavier than protons and I said last time that particles decay only into lighter versions of themselves. Technically that's true, but if the neutron is in a nucleus (which is usually is), it can actually borrow some of the nucleus' binding energy. Since energy is just unprocessed mass, this additional energy gives the particle the ability to decay:</p>\[ p \to n + e^+ + \nu_e \]

### Cosmic Ray Air Showers

Luckily, the universe is also very kind to us, so gives us a lot of high energy particles from outer space for free. And when I'm talking about high energy, I mean **really** high energy, like between \\( 10^9 \\) and \\(10^21\\) electron volts worth of energy. Since the energy is so high, this is more than enough energy for our particles to decay into more particles. And then those particles can decay into even more particles. And then *those* particles can decay into *even more* particles. And so on and so forth.

Let me walk you through the process. Most of the particles the universe gives us are protons. These high energy protons collide with oxygen and nitrogen, causing them to split into pions, neutrons and more protons.

The newly formed protons will also keep on splitting into pions, neutrons and more protons until they run out of energy.

The charged pions (\\( \\pi^+ \\) and \\( \\pi^- \\)) also decay into pions neutrons and protons, until they eventually have low enough energy to do the pion-to-muon think I spoke about last time, where they form muons and muon neutrinos. The muons could then decay into electrons, but if we are lucky, some of them reach the surface before they have time to, and hence we can capture muons.

The neutral pions (\\( \\pi^0 \\)) split into photons, which through pair production split into electrons and positrons, which then become photons, which through pair production split into electrons and positrons, which then become photons, which through pair production split into electrons and positrons...

This continual splitting of particles forms a cosmic ray air shower.

### The Sun

In addition, our big bright neighbour the sun also sends particles our way.

#### Solar Wind

The sun has a pretty freaky magnetic field. Although we don't really know how it works, it can somehow interact with the sun's plasma to create a giant space laser, aimed at planet Earth, shooting us with electrons, protons and alpha particles. Now this may seem scary, but don't worry, they aren't super energetic particles, only a couple thousand electron volts.

You'll get a more detailed explanation if you look into astronomy a bit more. But in short, the surface of the sun is pretty crazy. Sometimes the magnetic fields shoot off into space instead of looping back to the sun (forming darks spots on the sun called *coronal holes*). Sometimes magnetic field lines randomly align in a weird way, such that the sun just vomits out a bunch of mass (called *coronal mass ejections*, or CMEs for short). These cause plasma from the sun to be shot into the solar system.

I know like half a second ago I was talking about how this isn't anything to worry about since the particles aren't super energetic. Now I didn't exactly lie to you, but I did fib a little. It doesn't do much to US here on planet earth, since we have a magnetic field which directs the plasma to the poles (which creates the northern / southern lights). However for planets like Mars which had lost their magnetic fields, the solar winds was able to slowly but surely rob it of its atmosphere.

And even on Earth, CMEs can interfere with GPSs and electricity distribution.

#### Solar neutrinos

The sun also donates a lot of electron neutrinos, produced from the fusion processes which keeps it going. This gives a neutrino flux (the number of neutrinos which are sent through one squared centimetre per second) of about \\( 7 \\times 10^{10} \\text{neutrinos cm}^{-2}\\text{s}^{-1} \\). That's 70 *billion* neutrinos through a single square centimetre... per second?! That's a bloody lot, innit?

Having so many neutrinos coming our way really comes in handy, because it's one of the few ways we can get an inkling into the workings of the solar core. Slightly more relevant to this section though, this process has taught us a fair bit about neutrinos themselves. For example, it turns out that scientists are EXTREMELY greedy, such that despite having *billions* of neutrinos to work with, they were still expecting AT LEAST 30% more! Their greed sickens me...

This was dubbed the *solar neutrino problem*, and was eventually solved by stating that neutrinos (like the electron neutrinos from the sun) can change flavour (into muon and tauon neutrinos) as they travel from the sun to earth. Since the original experiment only detected electron neutrinos, they missed the remaining non-electron neutrinos which were detected later with better experiments. And since different flavoured neutrinos oscillate at different phases, and the phase difference is proportional to mass, this must mean that the neutrino flavours have some mass.

### TL:DR

Just so we are on the same page, you likely won't need to remember the amounts of energies or the fluxes, you just need a decent enough understanding of the process itself. With that in mind, here's what you'll need to know (probably):

- Beta minus decay produces protons, electrons and antineutrinos
- Beta plus decay produces neutrons, positrons and neutrinos, and can only take place in atoms where there is a high enough binding energy
- High energy particles from outer space (mostly protons) collide with oxygen and nitrogen in the atmosphere to produce more particles
- High energy photons produce neutrons, pions and lower energy protons
- High energy charged pions also become neutrons, protons and lower energy pions, and sometimes produce muons
- Neutral pions split into photons, which split into electrons and positrons (pair production), which become more photons, and so on and so forth
- Solar wind happens when the sun's magnetic field aligns in such a way that it aims solar plasma (containing protons, electrons and alpha particles) towards us
- The fusion processes in the sun produces *billions* of neutrinos
- Solar neutrinos give us incite into the inner workings of the sun's core, and tell us that neutrinos have non-zero mass

And remember: scientists are EXTREMELY greedy.
